Know your Pivot screen

1Menus. File has Save, Load Background Image, Create Text Object and Export Animation. Edit has Undo and Options.
2Timeline. Every frame you add appears here. Click a frame to see or change it.
3Play, Stop, Loop and the speed slider.
4Background picks a frame's background. Add Figure adds another figure.
5Selected figure buttons: delete, edit, centre, flip, colour, join, layer order, copy/paste, and the size box (100.00).
6Add Frame saves the current pose as a new frame.
7Canvas. Red handles bend a body part. The orange handle moves the whole figure.

Teacher notes

This sequence adapts Tasks 1, 2, 4, 6 and 8 from the Animation Activities document into a scaffolded progression:

  • Activity 1 is new. It covers the core mechanics students need before Task 1 (frames, handles, onion skins, playback speed).
  • Activity 2 combines Task 1 (falling from the top icon) and Task 2 (jumping safely from icon to icon).
  • Activity 3 combines Task 4 (Animate Your Name, minimum 30 frames) and Task 6 (in colour).
  • Activity 4 is Task 8 (short story using Character, Setting, Conflict and Resolution), extended to three scenes with their own backgrounds and a GIF/MP4 export. The original marking rubric is included.

The screen images are built from Pivot Animator 5 screenshots. The example animations play inside the replica window, and Activity 1 includes a working practice window. Each activity is designed for 1–2 lessons. Checklist ticks are saved only in the student's own browser.

Activity 1 · Animation basics

Wave & Walk

Your stick figure will raise an arm, wave hello, then walk across the screen. Animation is lots of still pictures (frames) shown quickly, one after another.

FramesHandlesOnion skinsPlayback speed
⏱ 1 lesson🎞 At least 20 frames
Learning goalI can make smooth movement by changing a figure a little bit in each frame.

What you'll make Press Play or Stop in the window, or click any frame

  1. Open Pivot and meet your figure

    Open Pivot Animator. A stick figure appears in the middle of the white frame area.

    • Drag a red handle to bend that body part. It swings around its joint, like a real elbow or knee.
    • Drag the orange handle to move the whole figure.

  2. Practise in the practice window

    This window works like the real Pivot. Drag the handles, then click Add Frame. Change the pose a little, add another frame, and repeat. Click Play to watch it and Stop to finish. The faded figure is the onion skin of your last frame.

    Click a frame in the timeline to change it, then click Update Frame. The red ✖ next to "Frame" deletes a frame.

  3. Frame 1: the starting pose

    Back in Pivot, pose your figure standing with both arms down. Drag the orange handle to move it to the left side of the frame area.

    Click Add Frame (or press Space). Frame 1 appears in the timeline at the top.

  4. Raise the arm, one small step at a time

    Drag the hand's red handle up a little and click Add Frame. Drag it up a little more and add another frame. Keep going until the arm is up (about 4 frames).

    The example on the right plays at one speed. The left arm has 3 frames, so it jumps. The right arm has 12 frames, so it moves smoothly.

  5. Use the onion skins

    Pivot shows a faded copy of your previous frame behind your figure. Use it to check that each move is small.

    To see several previous frames like the picture below, go to Edit → Options and set the number of onion skins (0–9).

  6. Wave hello

    Move the hand a little to one side and add a frame, then a little to the other side and add a frame. Repeat so the hand waves back and forth at least 3 times (6+ frames). Then lower the arm over 3–4 frames.

    Shortcut: hover over a handle and press L to put it back where it was in the previous frame.

  7. Walk across the screen

    A walk repeats the same few poses. For each frame:

    1. Drag the orange handle a small step to the right.
    2. Swing one leg forward and the other back.
    3. Swing the arms the opposite way to the legs.
    4. Click Add Frame.

    Keep going until your figure reaches the right side.

  8. Play it and adjust the speed

    1. Click Play to watch your animation.
    2. Drag the speed slider up or down to change how fast it plays.
    3. Click Stop when you're done.
    Looks jerky? Add more frames with smaller moves in between.

  9. Save your animation

    Go to File → Save Animation As… (Ctrl+Alt+S). Type the name below and click Save. Then copy the file into the hand-in work folder.

Activity 2 · Backgrounds

Desktop Jumper

Take a screenshot of your own desktop, load it into Pivot as a background, and make your stick figure jump safely from icon to icon down to the bottom of the screen.

FramesHandlesOnion skinsBackground imagesResizingJump arcs
⏱ 1–2 lessons🎞 At least 30 frames
Learning goalI can load a background and animate a figure so it moves realistically within a scene.

What you'll make Press Play or Stop in the window, or click any frame

  1. Arrange your desktop icons like stairs

    Minimise all your windows so you can see the desktop.

    1. Right-click an empty part of the desktop → View → untick Auto arrange icons.
    2. Drag your icons into a zig-zag staircase from the top of the screen to the bottom.

  2. Take a screenshot

    Press ⊞ Win + PrtScn. The screen flashes and the picture is saved in Pictures → Screenshots.

  3. Load the screenshot as a background

    1. Go to File → Load Background Image (Ctrl+B) and open your screenshot.

    2. Click Background and choose your screenshot for frame 1. Do this before adding more frames, because new frames copy the background.

  4. Shrink your figure to fit the icons

    Click your figure to select it. Type a smaller number in the size box (try 60) and press Enter, or hold Alt and drag a handle.

    Then drag the orange handle so the feet stand on top of the highest icon.

    Watch out: resize before you start animating, or your figure will suddenly change size.

  5. Plan one jump: crouch → arc → land

    1. Crouch (2 frames): bend the knees and swing the arms back.
    2. Arc (5–8 frames): move up and across, then down and across, like a rainbow shape. Tuck the legs.
    3. Land (2 frames): feet touch the next icon with bent knees, then stand up.

    Click through this jump frame by frame. The faded figure is the onion skin, just like in Pivot.

  6. Jump all the way down

    Repeat crouch → arc → land from icon to icon until your figure reaches the bottom of the screen. Do at least 3 jumps. Check that the feet land on top of each icon, not floating above it or sinking into it.

  7. Play, fix and save

    Play your animation and fix any jerky parts. Save with File → Save Animation As….

    No screenshot? Use File → Set Background Colour to make a gradient sky instead.

Activity 3 · Adding text

Animate Your Name

Make every letter of your name its own text object, then animate the letters so they drop in, bounce and dance in colour. A stick figure then walks in to introduce you.

FramesOnion skinsBackgroundsResizingText objectsColour & gradientsSpeech
⏱ 1–2 lessons🎞 At least 30 frames
Learning goalI can create text objects and animate them, along with figures, on a coloured background.

What you'll make

  1. Make a colourful background

    Start a new animation (File → New). Then go to File → Set Background Colour.

    • Pick a solid colour, a linear gradient or a radial gradient.
    • Choose your colours and click Add.

    A dark background with bright letters is easy to read.

  2. Create one text object for each letter

    Go to File → Create Text Object (Ctrl+T). In the Text Editor:

    1. Type one letter of your name.
    2. Choose a font and colour.
    3. Click Add.

    Repeat for every letter, so each letter can move on its own.

    Same letter twice? (like the Ls in "BELLA") Click the letter, then press Ctrl+D to duplicate it.

  3. Frame 1: hide the letters

    Text objects have an orange handle, just like stick figures. Drag each letter above the white frame area, into the grey part. Anything in the grey part won't be seen in your animation.

    Click Add Frame.

  4. Drop the letters in, one at a time

    Move the first letter down a little in each frame until it reaches its place, then start the next letter.

    • Bounce: go slightly past the spot, come back up a little, then settle.
    • Overlap: start the next letter before the last one finishes bouncing.

  5. Make your name dance in colour

    When all the letters are in place, make them move together for 8–10 frames: a wave, a spin (Alt+drag) or a grow and shrink.

    In colour (Task 6): click a letter, then click the colour button to change its colour every few frames.

  6. Add a character who speaks

    Click Add Figure to add a stick figure, and make it walk in under your name. Then create one more text object, such as "That's me!", and place it next to the figure's head.

  7. Check, play and save

    Your animation needs at least 30 frames. Play it through, then save with File → Save Animation As….

Activity 4 · Scenes & export

My Mini Story

Plan and animate a short story with three scenes. Each scene has its own background, there's text on screen, and at the end you export a GIF or video that anyone can watch.

FramesBackgroundsText objectsJumps & walksMultiple scenesStoryboardingExport GIF / MP4
⏱ 2–3 lessons🎞 At least 60 frames (about 20 per scene)
Learning goalI can plan a story and animate it across multiple scenes, then export it as a finished animation.

What you'll make Watch the background change in the timeline

  1. Plan your story

    Every story has a character, a setting, a conflict (a problem) and a resolution (how it gets solved). Fill in your plan. It's saved in this browser only, so use Copy my plan to paste it into a document.

    Scene 1 · Beginning

    Scene 2 · Middle (the problem)

    Scene 3 · End (the fix)

    Keep it friendly: no violence, and no characters getting hurt or falling off things.

  2. Load all three backgrounds

    Make or find one picture for each scene: draw it in Paint, use a screenshot, or use free-to-use images from your teacher.

    Go to File → Load Background Image once for each picture. All three will then be ready under the Background button.

  3. Animate Scene 1: the beginning

    On frame 1, click Background and choose your Scene 1 picture. Add a title text object (Ctrl+T), then animate the beginning of your story (about 20 frames).

  4. Change scene: switch the background

    When Scene 1 is finished, start Scene 2:

    1. Click Background and choose your Scene 2 picture.
    2. Move your character to where they start in the new place, and delete anything you don't need.
    3. Click Add Frame. New frames keep this background.

    Animate the problem, then do the same for Scene 3 and the resolution. Finish with a "The End" text object. In the timeline you'll see where each scene starts:

    Fix lots of frames at once: click the first frame, hold Shift and click the last frame. Choose the background, then click Update Frame.

  5. Check the whole story and save

    Play it all the way through. Does each scene make sense? Is the text on screen long enough to read? Set the speed slider carefully, because your export uses the same speed.

    Save your project with File → Save Animation As…. This .piv file is the one you can still edit.

  6. Export your finished animation

    1. Go to File → Export Animation and choose a format from the list that opens:
      • Animated GIF: plays in web browsers and slides.
      • Video (MP4): plays in video players, and you can add sound in a video editor.
    2. Type your file name and click Save.
    3. In the options window, leave the settings as they are and click Export.
    4. Wait for the progress bar to finish, then open the file to check it plays.
    Need to change something? An exported GIF or MP4 can't be edited. Open your .piv file, fix it, then export again.
